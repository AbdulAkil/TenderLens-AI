# Initial commercial validation

Positioning: TenderLens turns official procurement notices into a company-specific
shortlist with matching reasons and visible uncertainty, delivered on a schedule.
Initial interview segment: Belgian IT/software and cybersecurity SMEs. Do not claim
exclusive coverage, eligibility guarantees or validated precision.

## First 3–5 paying customers

1. Identify 10–15 businesses through existing professional contacts and opt-in referrals.
2. Ask how they currently find contracts, how often, which tenders waste time, what
   makes a bid impossible and who decides whether to pursue one.
3. Offer a short interview and a manually reviewed pilot shortlist based on their real
   capabilities. Keep national-source coverage limits explicit.
4. Review every opportunity together. Record useful/irrelevant/not eligible feedback,
   source gaps and what they would pay to receive the next digest.
5. Test the €29/€79/€149 hypotheses with actual willingness to pay. Complete the legal,
   invoicing and payment activation gates before accepting real charges.

The contact form persists pilot interest; no unsolicited marketing is sent automatically.
Internal outreach templates below are drafts, not sent messages.

## Interview prompts

- Tell me about the last public contract you considered. How did you find it?
- What did the initial screening cost in time, and what caused you to reject it?
- Which services/regions/requirements make an opportunity useful or impossible?
- What information must an email contain before you open the official notice?
- Would a reviewed weekly shortlist change your workflow? What would make it useless?
- Who approves a subscription, and what evidence would justify paying for it?

## Permission-based outreach draft

Subject: A procurement shortlist for [company]

Hello [name], I’m building TenderLens for Belgian SMEs that screen public contracts.
Would you be open to a 15-minute discussion about how [company] finds and rejects
tenders today? With your permission, I can prepare a small, source-linked example
using your actual services. It covers TED notices and does not promise full Belgian
coverage. No obligation or automatic email subscription.

## Onboarding draft

Your account is ready. Add your actual services, delivery regions and certifications,
then choose weekly or daily delivery where your plan permits it. Review the first
matches and mark anything irrelevant. Scores indicate relevance, not proven eligibility.

## Trial conversion draft

Your TenderLens trial is approaching its end. Review your saved opportunities and the
usefulness of the digest before deciding whether to continue. The proposed plans start
at €29/month excluding applicable VAT. Billing activation and invoice arrangements
must be completed before live payment. No award or revenue outcome is promised.

## Comparison

| Workflow | Official portal search | TenderLens pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Official notice authority | Original source | Links back to source |
| Company-specific screening | Manual | Deterministic, experimental relevance |
| Coverage | Portal-specific | TED + reviewed manual imports |
| Scheduled shortlist | Depends on portal settings | Reviewed daily/weekly digest |
| Eligibility decision | Business reviews source | Business reviews source |
| Notes/feedback | Internal process | Saved records and tenant feedback |

## Founder weekly rhythm

Review pipeline health and email failures daily. Review match quality with each pilot
weekly. Record signup/onboarding/account events without third-party tracking; measure
first useful match through explicit customer feedback, not a high score alone. Track
trial-to-paid, cancellation, provider spending and support time. MRR/gross margin
calculations require actual paid subscriptions and complete costs; none are claimed yet.
